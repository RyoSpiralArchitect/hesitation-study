# 001 · A wrong choice can carry different levels of commitment

**Observed 22–24 November 2026.** The same GPT-4.1 mini snapshot answered 640 Japanese multiple-choice questions and their English translations. Ordinary answers, discretionary hesitation, uncertainty only when warranted, assertive answers and source-only rewrites each contributed two outputs per question in each language. The English trial followed the Japanese one by two days.

Within the pairs in which the ordinary and discretionary-hesitation conditions selected the **same wrong option**, mean self-reported probability of being correct moved from 90.7% to 79.0% in Japanese (216 pairs) and from 91.3% to 80.5% in English (220 pairs). Even the number elicited as confidence responded to the instruction.

Rewriting an existing answer gave a clearer view of form. Of 1,270 scorable rewrites in each language, 1,269 Japanese outputs matched a pause or filler marker. The sole English match was an ellipsis inside an arithmetic expression. In English, 1,269 rewrites contained an epistemic hedge. The discretionary generation instruction and the direct rewrite instruction differed in force, leaving open how much of the difference followed from wording and how much followed from editing an existing answer.

After resolving formatting failures where the chosen option was apparent, the hesitation effect on all-output accuracy was +0.23 percentage points in Japanese and +0.31 in English. The paired English-minus-Japanese difference was +0.08 points (95% interval −2.19 to +2.27). Choice constraints, lexical scoring and translation limit the interpretation.

**日本語要約**　同じ誤選択肢を出したときにも、事前指示で言い方と自己申告確率が動いた。書き換えでは、日本語は「えっと」のような表現、英語は “I think” のような留保を多く使った。選択肢を外した場合と、編集時と初答時で同じ指示を与えた場合は、この実験からは分からない。
